# Settle Landing — Design Guidelines

## Design Context

### Users
Power users who choose tools deliberately. They are keyboard-first, value their attention, and are wary of busy, oversold software-marketing pages. When they land here they want to quickly understand what Settle is, see honest screenshots, and either download or close the tab. They are likely to spot — and dislike — generic AI-generated aesthetics, so the page itself is part of the product's credibility.

### Brand Personality
**Calm, focused, considered.** A tool that respects your attention. Quiet confidence, no urgency, no marketing voice. The landing page should feel like it was made by the same person who made the app — not by a growth team. Short sentences, plain language, no exclamation marks, no "transform your workflow" energy.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Monochrome technical** — software-craft minimalism in the lineage of Linear, Vercel, and Resend.

- Near-monochrome palette with **one** restrained accent (not a gradient).
- Tight, deliberate grid. Precise spacing. Visible rhythm.
- A geometric sans for UI; a monospace for technical details (keys, code, file paths). No display serifs.
- Both light and dark themes, switched via `prefers-color-scheme` — no in-page toggle.
- Decoration earns its place or it doesn't appear. Nothing is there "to fill space."

**Anti-references — explicitly avoid these (all flagged as AI-slop):**
1. **Glassmorphism / blurred panels.** No `backdrop-filter: blur`, no frosted cards, no semi-transparent stacks.
2. **Animated gradient blobs.** No drifting radial gradients in the hero. No floating colored shapes.
3. **Neon gradient text accents.** No `background-clip: text` rainbow words. The accent color appears as a solid, sparingly.
4. **Generic 3-column feature card grids.** No row of six identical bordered cards with stroke-SVG icons. Features should be presented in a way that reflects what they actually are — possibly a list, possibly inline with copy, possibly with a single anchoring screenshot instead of icons.

### Design Principles

1. **Quiet beats loud.** When in doubt, remove. The page should feel like a held breath, not a sales pitch. Whitespace, restraint, and typographic rhythm do the work that gradients and glow used to do.
2. **One accent, used sparingly.** A single quiet accent color (think Linear's purple, Vercel's nothing-but-black, Resend's soft blue) — applied to links, focused states, and at most one anchor moment per page. Never as a gradient.
3. **Type carries the design.** Hierarchy comes from size, weight, and spacing — not from boxes, borders, or backgrounds. A good monospace earns its place for keyboard shortcuts and technical details; the rest is a clean geometric sans.
4. **Honest surfaces.** No fake depth. No backdrop blur, no glow halos behind screenshots, no inset highlight gradients pretending to be light. Borders are 1px and matter-of-fact. Shadows, if used at all, are soft and singular.
5. **Show the app, don't decorate it.** Screenshots are the hero. Frame them simply (or not at all), let them be the proof. The page around them should recede.
6. **Both themes, equally considered.** Light and dark are not toggled in-page — they follow OS preference. Each must be designed, not auto-inverted. Light is the default mental model when designing; dark is its careful translation.
7. **Accessibility is part of "quiet."** WCAG AA contrast minimums; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (already partially in place); real focus rings; semantic HTML; keyboard navigation that mirrors the app's own keyboard-first ethos.
